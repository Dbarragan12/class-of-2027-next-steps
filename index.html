Para las familias

For parents and guardians — la versión en español

Su estudiante necesita su ayuda con tres cosas este año. No necesita hablar inglés ni haber ido a la universidad para ayudar con ninguna de las tres.

  • El formulario de ayuda financiera (FAFSA o WASFA), en octubre. Se necesitan sus impuestos de hace dos años. Es gratis. Abre casi todo lo demás en esta página. Si su familia es indocumentada, el formulario correcto es el WASFA — es del estado de Washington, no del gobierno federal, y no se comparte con inmigración.
  • Las fechas límite. La mayoría del dinero se decide entre octubre y marzo del último año. Pregúntele a su estudiante una vez al mes: «¿A cuáles solicitaste?»
  • Su historia familiar. Muchas becas de aquí — del valle, de la industria de la fruta, de la Nación Yakama, para familias trabajadoras del campo — se otorgan por quién es su familia y de dónde viene. Cuéntele esa historia a su estudiante. Es parte de la solicitud.

Nunca se paga por solicitar una beca. Si alguien le pide dinero, es fraude.

Section 02 · Common App: Writing

Your story, in 650 words or fewer.

The Common App personal statement goes to every Common App college you select. Your job is not to sound impressive. Your job is to make the reader understand something real about how you notice, think, choose, change, or contribute.

The most useful rule: choose the story before the prompt. A strong essay often fits several prompts; prompt 7 can hold almost any meaningful personal statement.

2026–27 limit: 650 words maximum. Aim for a complete essay, usually 500–650 words.

The seven choices

Choose a doorway—not a formula.

Open each prompt for a plain-language translation and a question that helps you test whether you have a real story.

Find the story first

Specific beats dramatic.

A small, sharply observed moment can reveal more than a life summary. Use this private story finder to collect scenes, details, and values before drafting.

Pick a structure

One turning point—or several connected moments?

Narrative

One experience changes something

Use this when one challenge, event, relationship, or realization has a clear before, turning point, action, and after. Spend more space on what you did and understood than on the problem.

Montage

Several moments share one thread

Use this when an object, place, habit, language, responsibility, or question connects multiple snapshots. Each scene should reveal a different side of you while returning to the same thread.

Draft here

Write in your own voice.

Sound like the thoughtful version of yourself—not a thesaurus. Use concrete nouns, exact actions, and details only you would know.

Revision test

Make the essay reveal a person.

    Ask for feedback this way: “What three words describe me after reading this? Where did you feel closest to me? Where did the essay become general or confusing?” Keep feedback that helps the essay sound more like you.

    AI boundary: use AI to ask you follow-up questions, find vague sentences, or check grammar. Do not let it invent the experience or replace your phrasing. Authentic voice is one of the two biggest things that makes an essay stand out.
