Section 04 · Pay for School

How will we pay for school or training?

Start with FAFSA or WASFA, check Washington College Grant, compare the real remaining cost, and use scholarships to close the gap.

Financial aid

Do I complete FAFSA or WASFA?

Do not enter citizenship or immigration details into this website. Use the official questionnaire or ask a trained adult privately.

I can file FAFSA, or I think I can → FAFSAFAFSA can connect eligible students to federal, Washington, and college aid. I cannot file FAFSA, I do not know, or I prefer not to → Check WASFAUse WSAC's official questionnaire. WASFA applies for Washington state aid, not federal aid.

A parent contributor without a Social Security number can still create the account needed for an eligible student's FAFSA. Use the official account instructions instead of assuming the student cannot apply.

Not sure? That is normal. Do not guess. Ask Luis Gutierrez, UW GEAR UP, your assigned advisory teacher, or a financial-aid office privately.

Money students often miss

Washington College Grant

For a family of four, $83,500 or less may mean a full WA Grant.

For 2026–27, a family of four may receive some WA Grant with income up to about $139,500. Household size, income, program, and annual rules affect the real award.

WA Grant can help pay for college, career training, supplies, living expenses, and approved apprenticeships. Apply through FAFSA or WASFA.

Check the official income and award chart →

College Bound Scholarship

If you were enrolled in College Bound, check your status and complete FAFSA or WASFA during senior year.

Check College Bound →

Passport to Careers

Foster-care and unaccompanied-homeless-youth support can help with college, training, or apprenticeships.

Check eligibility →

Some colleges also require CSS Profile

Some private colleges use CSS Profile in addition to FAFSA. Check each college's aid page. Eligible domestic undergraduate students can receive a fee waiver.

Check CSS Profile →

Compare the real remaining cost

A larger scholarship does not always mean a lower bill. Compare grants, scholarships, work-study, loans, housing, food, fees, travel, and what your family must actually pay.

Compare aid offers →

Application fee waivers

Common App and many colleges offer fee waivers. Before paying, ask Luis Gutierrez, College & Career Specialist, for help requesting the correct waiver.

Federal loans are borrowed money

Grants and scholarships usually do not need repayment. Loans do. Compare federal loan terms before considering private loans.

Understand federal loans →

Verification is a follow-up, not a rejection

A college may ask for documents after FAFSA or WASFA. Watch the college portal and email, respond quickly, and ask before sending sensitive information.

Review and correct FAFSA →

Keep aid active after admission

Complete orientation, registration, satisfactory-progress rules, and any missing financial-aid tasks. Being admitted does not finish the money process.

Section 06 · Writing

One strong story. One clear process.

Use this writing coach for a college personal statement, a scholarship essay, or a short answer. It helps you choose a story, build an outline, draft in your own voice, and check your work before you submit.

What strong readers are looking for: A real person answering the exact question with specific evidence, thoughtful reflection, and their own voice. There is no “perfect” topic and no formula that guarantees admission or a scholarship.

        College short answers, “Why us?” and Additional Information
        
      
          
      Short answer
      Answer in the first sentence. Use one example, not a miniature life story. Every sentence should add evidence or meaning.
      
          
      “Why this college or program?”
      Name two or three specific opportunities and explain what you would do with them. Connect each one to something you have already done, learned, or want to investigate. Prestige, weather, and “a beautiful campus” are not enough.
      
          
      Additional Information
      This is not a second personal essay. Use it only when useful context—such as a schedule change, family responsibility, health issue, school limitation, or unusual transcript detail—would otherwise be misunderstood. Clear facts are enough, and it is okay to leave it blank.

      Section 03 · College Requirements
      
    
      What do colleges and programs look for?
      
    
      Use this page from the first visit to the final enrollment step. Open each section to see what students and families should prepare—and what can be different by school, major, or program.
      
  
      
  
      
    
      
      
      
        1. Confirm you are on track to graduate
        
      
          
      Washington seniors need a current High School and Beyond Plan and at least one locally offered graduation pathway aligned with that plan. Pathway options vary by district, so do not assume a test, course, or activity automatically completed yours.
      
          
      • Ask: Which graduation pathway am I completing?
      • Ask whether it is recorded and whether your High School and Beyond Plan is current.
      • Confirm every remaining credit, local requirement, and deadline with counseling.
      
          Washington graduation requirements →
        
      
      
      
      
      
        2. Choose the kind of program first
        
      
          
      A four-year university, YVC, Perry Tech, and an apprenticeship do not ask for the same things. Before you start an application, write down the school, program or major, start term, and application type.
      
          
      • Four-year college: review high-school preparation, grades, application writing, and major-specific admission.
      • YVC or another two-year college: plan for admission, placement, orientation, registration, and a pathway choice.
      • Technical school or apprenticeship: check program prerequisites, assessments, interviews, start dates, and whether paid training is part of the program.
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        3. Check your high-school classes and credits
        
      
          
      For Washington four-year admission, compare your transcript with the College Academic Distribution Requirements (CADRs). The common checklist is:
      
          
      • 4 credits of English
      • 3 credits of mathematics, including Algebra II or higher preparation
      • 1 senior-year math-based quantitative course
      • 3 credits of science, including lab science requirements
      • 3 credits of social sciences or social studies
      • 2 credits of world language
      • About 1 credit of fine, visual, or performing arts, depending on the school
      
          
      Course names matter. An applied or remedial class may not meet a college-admission requirement even if it counts toward high-school graduation. Ask your advisory teacher or counseling to compare your transcript with the exact college page.
      
          UW CADR details → · WSU CADR details →
          
      Claim college credit you may already have earned
      
          
      Running Start, College in the High School, AP, and some CTE Dual Credit courses can create college credit or a separate college transcript. Ask your teacher or counselor what credit was available, whether you completed the required registration, and which college holds the record.
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        4. Understand GPA, grades, and course choices
        
      
          
      There is no single GPA that guarantees admission everywhere. Colleges may review your full transcript, grade trend, senior schedule, course difficulty, CADRs, and the preparation expected for your major. A strong senior schedule still matters after you apply.
      
          
      • Keep your grades steady or improving.
      • Do not drop a course without checking how it affects graduation and college requirements.
      • Ask each school whether the major has a separate GPA or prerequisite rule.
      • Send the final official transcript if you enroll; an application self-report is not always the final transcript.
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        5. Know when SAT or ACT matters
        
      
          
      Do not assume every school has the same testing rule. UW says SAT/ACT scores are not required for admission and are not used in its normal holistic review, but exceptions and other uses can exist. Another college, scholarship, athletic program, homeschool situation, or placement process may have different rules.
      
          
      • Open the official test-policy page for every school before paying for a test or sending scores.
      • Ask whether scores affect scholarships, course placement, NCAA eligibility, or a special program.
      • International applicants may have separate English-proficiency requirements.
      • Military exploration: the ASVAB can help students learn about military career qualifications. Taking it does not enlist anyone. Ask THS counseling about the next school testing date, and review official ASVAB information →.
      
          UW test-score guidance →
        
      
      
      
      
      
        6. Prepare the application materials
        
      
          
      Set up one personal email address you will keep after graduation. Then prepare:
      
          
      • Personal and family information requested by the application
      • High-school course names, grades, and dates
      • Activities, jobs, family responsibilities, and service
      • College personal statement or short-answer writing
      • Recommendation information only when the school or program asks for it
      • Application fee or fee-waiver plan
      • A list of every account, confirmation number, deadline, and missing item
      • Transcript: ask THS counseling or the College & Career Center early how to send an official transcript. A college may still require it even after you self-report grades in an application.
      • Fee waiver: before paying an application fee, see Luis Gutierrez, College & Career Specialist. Students may also ask UW GEAR UP or their assigned advisory teacher for help. Cost should not stop a student from applying.
      
          UW first-year application checklist →
        
      
      
      
      
      
        7. Know the YVC steps before registration
        
      
          
      YVC is not “apply once and show up.” New students should plan for this sequence:
      
          
      1. Apply for admission and receive a ctcLink student ID.
      2. Complete English and math placement by testing or sending transcripts for evaluation.
      3. Choose a pathway and watch for the New Student Orientation invitation.
      4. Attend orientation, meet with advising, and register for classes.
      5. Check your financial-aid account, tuition balance, and payment plan before the due date.
      
          YVC new-student steps → · YVC orientation and dates →
        
      
      
      
      
      
        8. Check program-specific requirements
        
      
          
      A school may admit you while a program still has another process. Nursing, dental hygiene, teaching, engineering, health programs, arts programs, and technical programs can have prerequisites, assessments, interviews, portfolios, auditions, background checks, or limited seats.
      
          
      Perry Tech, for example, may require an entrance assessment for the selected program, proof of high-school completion, identification, and a financial plan. Ask for the exact program checklist—not just the general school application.
      
          Perry Tech enrollment steps → · Perry Tech assessment information →
        
      
      
      
      
      
        9. Do not stop after you are admitted
        
      
          
      Admission is not the last step. Before the first day, students may still need to:
      
          
      • Accept the admission offer by the stated date
      • Send a final transcript or other required records
      • Complete FAFSA/WASFA verification and compare the real remaining cost
      • Pay a deposit, arrange housing or transportation, and accept or decline aid
      • Complete placement, orientation, advising, registration, immunization, or program paperwork
      • Check the student portal and school email every week
      
          
      Keep screenshots or confirmation emails for every completed step.
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        10. Family and student final check
        
      
          
      • We know the exact school, program, and start term.
      • We checked the required high-school classes and GPA or prerequisite rules.
      • We know whether SAT/ACT, placement, an assessment, or an interview is required.
      • We have the application accounts, personal email, transcript information, and writing materials.
      • We know the financial-aid form, scholarship steps, and remaining-cost plan.
      • We wrote down the next deadline and who to contact when something is unclear.
      
          
      Still unsure? Start with your assigned advisory teacher, UW GEAR UP, or Luis Gutierrez in the College & Career Center and share the exact school, program, or form. You do not need to figure it out alone.
