College is one path — not the only path

Get hired. Get trained. Get paid.

These are apprenticeship and earn-while-you-learn options students can explore within roughly 100 miles of Toppenish. Some lead to licenses or journey-level credentials; others are employer-paid training programs that can start right after high school.

The idea

Sometimes they pay you to learn.

An apprenticeship combines structured training with real work. A four-year college degree can be the right route for many careers, but it is not the only way to build a skilled, well-paid career.

Apprenticeship

You work, receive supervised training and related instruction, earn wages while learning, and work toward a recognized credential or license.

Employer-paid training

An employer hires you into a structured training role. It may not always be a registered apprenticeship, so check what credential you earn and what happens after training.

Local opportunities

Start with these.

These examples are close enough to Toppenish to make the possibility feel real. Requirements, openings and pay can change, so verify each live listing.

Healthcare · Yakima

Medical Assistant Apprentice

Paid on-the-job trainingHigh school/GED pathway

Yakima Valley Farm Workers Clinic offers a Medical Assistant Apprentice program in Yakima. The program includes 2,000 hours of paid on-the-job training, supplemental online coursework, and in-person lab practice.

  • Full-time employer-based pathway
  • Learn alongside experienced medical assistants
  • Designed to build toward medical-assisting credentials

Heavy equipment · Ellensburg

Operating Engineers Apprenticeship

2026 application openDeadline Nov. 6, 2026

The Operating Engineers Regional Training Program in Ellensburg trains apprentices for careers involving heavy equipment and related skilled work.

  • 2026 application is currently open
  • Application and required documents due before 5 p.m. Nov. 6, 2026
  • High school diploma, GED or equivalent documentation is part of the application process

Advanced manufacturing · Yakima

AJAC Production Technician — Youth

$0 tuition$0 fees$0 books

AJAC's registered youth apprenticeship teaches students to work with advanced manufacturing machinery using mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic and computer technologies.

  • Yakima-area locations include YV-Tech, Yakima schools, West Valley and Yakima Valley College
  • Career Bridge lists a journey-level credential
  • Can give students a bridge from high school into skilled manufacturing

Money students may miss

Washington can help pay apprenticeship expenses, too.

For 2026–27, the WA Grant for Apprenticeship lists a maximum award of $2,548 for eligible apprentices in participating programs. Eligibility and actual awards depend on income and the apprenticeship sponsor.
